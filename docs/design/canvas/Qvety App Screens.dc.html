Qvety  /  Interface  /  Phone

The phone is not a narrow desktop

Staff use their own phones, mostly away from the desk: a vet checking a record in the consult room, a receptionist sending a recall from the corridor. Four rules follow from that. Tables become stacked rows, because a six-column table cannot be read at 390 px. The sidebar becomes a five-item bottom bar, because a thumb reaches the bottom of a phone and not the top. Every target is at least 44 px. And the 32 px brand strip stays 32 px — it is chrome, not a control — with a separate 48 px title bar under it carrying the page name and its one action.

نسيت كلمة المرور؟ العربية · English
Tables become rows

One row per appointment: time in a fixed leading column at 15 px / 600, patient and owner stacked in the middle, state tag pinned to the trailing edge. The six desktop columns collapse to three pieces of information, chosen because those are the three a receptionist reads out loud.

Sidebar becomes a bottom bar

The same five destinations, 60 px tall, icon over a 10 px label. The active item keeps the desktop treatment — primary-light fill with a 3 px bar — moved from the leading edge to the top edge of the cell.

Primary action is anchored

On record and invoice screens the buttons sit in a fixed 48 px bar at the bottom of the viewport, above the scroll. A vet holding a cat in one hand should not have to scroll to find "New visit".

Sizes that change

Body goes 14 → 15 px in list rows, inputs to 16 px so iOS does not zoom on focus, and every target to 44 px minimum. The 32 px brand strip and the type scale everywhere else stay exactly as specified.
